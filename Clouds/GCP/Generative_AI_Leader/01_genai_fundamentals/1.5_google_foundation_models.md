# 1.5 Google's Foundation Model Family

Google offers a family of foundation models, each designed for different purposes, modalities, and deployment contexts. Understanding the distinctions between these models — not at a technical level, but at the level of "which model fits which business need" — is essential for making informed decisions about AI deployments.

This document covers the four primary models in Google's foundation model family: Gemini, Gemma, Imagen, and Veo.

---

## Gemini
Gemini is Google's flagship foundation model — its most capable, most versatile AI system. Designed from the ground up to work natively with multiple types of content simultaneously, Gemini can understand and generate text, images, audio, video, and code in an integrated way. It is not a text model with image capabilities added on — it was architected as a multimodal system from the start.

Gemini is accessible through the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (for enterprise application development), through Google AI Studio (for developer prototyping), and embedded in consumer and enterprise products like the Gemini app and Gemini for Google Workspace.

---

### The Gemini Model Family
Google releases Gemini in multiple variants, each optimized for different trade-offs between capability, speed, and cost. The model family evolves rapidly — new versions and variants are released frequently, so treat the versions below as representative of the tiers rather than a fixed list. As of mid-2026, the latest flagship release is **Gemini 3.1 Pro**, with Gemini 3.5 Pro/Flash still widely in production use.

**Gemini 3.1 Pro (latest flagship)**: The most capable model in the family as of 2026, optimized for the most demanding reasoning tasks. Use this when accuracy, depth, and handling complex, multi-step problems matters more than speed or cost. Well-suited for:
- Complex legal and financial document analysis
- Long-context reasoning (processing entire books, large codebases, extensive research archives)
- Sophisticated multi-step problem solving
- Tasks where the quality of reasoning is critical and per-query cost is acceptable

**Gemini 3.5 Flash (production workhorse)**: Optimized for speed and cost efficiency, with strong capability for most practical tasks. This is typically the right choice for production workloads where we need to balance performance with economics. Well-suited for:
- High-volume customer-facing applications
- Real-time processing where latency matters
- Cost-sensitive automation at scale
- Most standard enterprise use cases where exceptional complexity is not required

**Gemini Nano**: A compact version of Gemini designed to run on-device — on smartphones, laptops, and edge hardware — without an internet connection. Sacrifices some capability for the dramatic reduction in compute required to run locally.

Well-suited for:
- Mobile and desktop applications where cloud latency is unacceptable
- Privacy-sensitive on-device processing (data never leaves the device)
- Always-available features that do not depend on network connectivity
- Embedded AI in devices with limited power budgets

---

### Native Multimodality: What It Means in Practice
The phrase "natively multimodal" describes how Gemini was trained — with text, images, audio, video, and code all interleaved during training, not processed separately. This has practical consequences for capability:

**Fluid cross-modal reasoning:** Gemini can analyze a photograph and discuss it in the same response that includes code and text. It does not need to "hand off" the image to a separate image model — it reasons about all of it together.

**Integrated understanding:** Show Gemini a chart alongside its accompanying text explanation, and it can understand both the visual data and the textual context simultaneously, as a human reader would.

**Practical example:** A user uploads a 50-slide presentation and asks Gemini to summarize the key arguments, identify any data slides that seem to contradict the text conclusions, and draft a set of discussion questions. This requires reading text, analyzing charts (images), and reasoning about the relationship between them — all in one task.

---

### Gemini's Long Context Window
One of Gemini's distinctive capabilities is its very large context window. Context window is the amount of content the model can consider at once:

| Context Window | What It Enables |
|---------------|----------------|
| 8K tokens (~6,000 words) | A few pages of a document |
| 128K tokens (~95,000 words) | A full book chapter or lengthy contract |
| 1M tokens (~750,000 words) | An entire novel, a large codebase, months of email archives |
| 2M tokens (~1.5 million words) | Multiple books, very large document collections |

Gemini 2.5 Pro and later models support context windows of over 1 million tokens — large enough to process entire books, extensive codebases, or large collections of corporate documents in a single interaction.

**Business implications of a large context window:**
- Process an entire contract (not just excerpts) for comprehensive review
- Analyze a complete year of customer support transcripts in one pass
- Provide an AI assistant with the full context of a complex, ongoing project
- Review an entire codebase when debugging or planning a major refactor

---

### Gemini's Key Strengths

- **State-of-the-art reasoning:** Consistently top performance on reasoning benchmarks
- **Long context:** Ability to work with very large volumes of content in a single interaction
- **Native multimodality:** Integrated understanding of text, images, audio, video, and code
- **Google ecosystem integration:** Deep integration with Google Workspace, Google Search, and Google Cloud
- **Grounding with Google Search:** Can be connected to real-time Google Search results, dramatically reducing hallucination for factual questions

---

### When to Choose Gemini

- When we need maximum capability for complex reasoning or multimodal tasks
- When we are building applications that integrate with Google Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets)
- When we need access to real-time information via Google Search grounding
- When long-context processing (entire large documents or collections) is required
- When our use case requires the best available performance and we have the budget for a premium model

## Gemma
Gemma is Google's family of **open-weight** foundation models — foundation models whose trained weights are publicly released and freely available for anyone to download, run, and modify. Gemma models are built using the same research and some of the same training approaches as Gemini, but are released openly rather than kept proprietary.

The name reflects the relationship: Gemma (from the Latin for "gem" or "jewel") is lighter and more accessible, built from the same research lineage as Gemini.

---

### What "Open-Weight" Means — and Why It Matters
This is the single most important thing to understand about Gemma: when you use Gemma, **you run the model yourself, on your own infrastructure**. Nothing is sent to Google's servers.

Compare the two approaches:

| | Gemini (via API) | Gemma (open-weight) |
|--|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Where does my data go?** | Sent to Google's servers to be processed | Never leaves your environment |
| **Who controls the infrastructure?** | Google | You |
| **What do I pay?** | Per token / per request | Only the compute to run it |
| **Can I modify the model?** | No (limited to fine-tuning via API) | Yes — fully customize the weights |
| **Who handles updates?** | Google | You |
| **Regulatory compliance** | Depends on Google's certifications | You control the entire stack |

For organizations with strict data governance requirements — regulated industries, government agencies, organizations with contractual obligations prohibiting cloud processing of specific data — open-weight models like Gemma are often the only viable path to using AI on sensitive data.

---

### Gemma Variants
The latest release as of 2026 is **Gemma 4**, continuing Google's commitment to releasing capable open-weight models alongside its proprietary offerings. The family includes multiple size variants to match different infrastructure budgets.

**Gemma (core):** Available in multiple sizes, balancing capability against the compute required to run it. Smaller versions can run on consumer hardware; larger versions require server-grade GPUs.

**CodeGemma:** Fine-tuned from Gemma specifically for code understanding and generation tasks — writing code, explaining code, completing code, finding bugs. Particularly strong for programming assistant use cases.

**PaliGemma:** A vision-language model — it accepts both images and text as input, enabling visual question answering, image captioning, and image-based reasoning tasks.

**ShieldGemma:** Safety classifiers built on the Gemma architecture, designed for detecting harmful content. Used as a safety layer within AI systems.

---

### Business Use Cases for Gemma

**Privacy-sensitive applications:** A healthcare network needs to process clinical notes to extract diagnosis codes, but HIPAA requirements and organizational policy prohibit patient data from leaving their private cloud environment. They deploy Gemma on their own compute infrastructure — the model processes clinical text on their servers, results stay in their environment, and no data touches a cloud AI API.

**Highly regulated industries:** A financial services firm developing AI for trading strategy analysis faces regulatory restrictions on where trading data can be processed. An open-weight model deployed on dedicated, regulated infrastructure is compliant in a way that a cloud API might not be.

**Proprietary data fine-tuning:** A manufacturing company wants to fine-tune a model on decades of proprietary maintenance records and failure analysis reports — their entire institutional knowledge of equipment behavior. With an open-weight model, they can fine-tune on this data in their own secure environment, with full control over what data is used and where it goes.

**Academic and research use:** Researchers can download Gemma weights, experiment with the model architecture, and publish findings — something not possible with proprietary models whose weights are hidden.

**Organizations building AI products:** Companies building AI-powered products may choose Gemma to avoid passing their customers' data through a third-party API and to have full control over the model serving infrastructure.

---

### Gemma's Trade-offs
Gemma's open-weight models are smaller and less capable than Gemini's top-tier models — this is an inherent trade-off of running a model on infrastructure we can afford to own or manage. For many practical tasks, Gemma's performance is excellent. For the most demanding reasoning tasks, Gemini's larger proprietary models will perform better.

Additionally, deploying and managing our own model infrastructure requires ML engineering expertise. This is a meaningful operational cost that organizations must account for. Using Gemini via API means Google handles model updates, infrastructure management, and scaling. Using Gemma on our own infrastructure means our team is responsible for all of that.

---

### When to Choose Gemma

- When data governance, regulatory compliance, or organizational policy prohibits sending data to an external cloud provider
- When we need full control over the processing environment
- When we want to fine-tune extensively on proprietary data with no data leaving our environment
- When we want to avoid per-token pricing and prefer to own the infrastructure cost
- When we have the engineering team to deploy and manage model serving infrastructure

## Imagen
Imagen is Google's text-to-image generation model family. We describe an image in natural language, and Imagen generates a photorealistic or stylistically specific image matching our description. Beyond generation from scratch, Imagen also supports image editing, style transfer, and variation generation.

The current generation is **Imagen 4** (generally available as of May 2025). Imagen 4 is offered in three variants — **Imagen 4 Fast** (approximately 2.7 seconds per image, optimized for cost and speed), **Imagen 4 Standard**, and **Imagen 4 Ultra** (highest quality output). All generated images are automatically watermarked with Google's **SynthID** technology, enabling AI-generated content to be identified and authenticated.

---

### How It Works (Conceptually)
Imagen uses diffusion model architecture. At a conceptual level: the model was trained on hundreds of millions of image-text pairs — photographs with captions, illustrations with descriptions, art with titles — until it learned the visual patterns of the world well enough to produce new images that match text descriptions. Given the description "a golden retriever puppy sitting in a field of sunflowers, morning light, photorealistic," Imagen has learned what each of those elements looks like and how they should combine in a compelling, realistic image.

The key capabilities:

**Text-to-image generation:** Describe what we want, get an image. The description can be highly specific (photorealistic, a specific style, particular lighting) or general and open-ended.

**Image editing from text instructions:** Provide an existing image and a text instruction — "change the background to a mountain range" or "remove the background" — and Imagen modifies the image accordingly.

**Image variation generation:** Generate multiple variations of an image concept — useful when we want to explore different interpretations of a visual idea.

**Background generation and removal:** Remove backgrounds from product images, or generate contextually appropriate backgrounds.

---

### Business Use Cases for Imagen

**Marketing and advertising:** A retail brand needs product images showing their furniture in different room settings. Instead of expensive photography shoots for each configuration, a visual content team uses Imagen to generate lifestyle images of each piece in dozens of different settings. What previously required weeks of scheduling, staging, and shooting can be produced in hours.

**Product visualization:** An e-commerce company uses Imagen to show products in contexts that do not yet exist — a new chair model in 15 different room styles before the physical product is even manufactured, enabling market testing of designs before production investment.

**Content marketing:** A content marketing team needs dozens of custom illustrations for blog posts, social media, and email campaigns every month. Imagen provides on-demand illustration generation, reducing dependence on stock photo subscriptions and enabling truly bespoke visuals.

**Concept exploration and prototyping:** A design team uses Imagen to quickly generate visual concepts for review — exploring dozens of design directions in a single session before committing to a direction for more detailed work.

---

### When to Choose Imagen

- When our use case involves generating or editing images from text descriptions
- Marketing and advertising teams that need high volumes of custom visual content
- Product visualization for e-commerce, design, or manufacturing
- Any workflow currently dependent on stock photography or hiring designers for routine visual content
- When we want to embed image generation capabilities in a custom application

## Veo
Veo is Google's video generation model — a system that generates video clips from text prompts or images. It represents the frontier of generative AI, applying similar principles to video that image generation models apply to still images, but with the added complexity of temporal consistency (making sure each frame flows naturally from the previous one).

The current generation is **Veo 3.1** (released October 2025), with **Veo 3.1 Lite** also available for cost-efficiency. Veo 3.1 is a landmark release: it is the first model to generate video and audio together natively, producing synchronized dialogue, sound effects, and ambient audio alongside the video frames. Output quality reaches up to 4K at 24fps, with clips of 4–8 seconds per generation. A **Veo 3.1 Lite** variant provides a more economical option for workflows that do not require maximum quality.

---

### What Veo Can Do
**Text-to-video generation:** Describe a video scene in text and Veo generates a video clip matching that description. The description can specify visual content, style (cinematic, documentary, animated), movement, and lighting.

**Native audio generation:** Unlike earlier video generation models that produced silent clips requiring audio to be added in post-production, Veo 3.1 generates synchronized audio alongside the video — dialogue, sound effects, music, and ambient noise all produced in a single generation pass.

**Image-to-video:** Provide a still image and describe the motion or development we want, and Veo animates the image into a video clip.

**Style and atmosphere control:** Specify visual styles, color grades, camera movements, and moods that the generated video should reflect.

---

### Business Use Cases for Veo

**Marketing video production:** A consumer brand needs short video clips for social media campaigns — product demonstrations, lifestyle content, seasonal variations. Veo can generate video content from creative briefs, dramatically reducing the cost and time of video production for short-form content.

**Product demonstrations:** An e-commerce platform wants to show each product in motion — a piece of furniture being used, clothing being worn, a kitchen appliance in action. Traditional video production for each product variation would be prohibitively expensive. AI video generation makes this economically feasible.

**Training and instructional content:** Organizations creating internal training content can generate illustrative video clips to accompany written or narrated training materials, adding visual context without expensive production.

**Concept visualization and storyboarding:** Creative directors and brand strategists use Veo to rapidly visualize campaign concepts before committing to expensive production — showing stakeholders how a concept would look as a video before any shoot is scheduled.

---

### Current State and Trajectory
Video generation is among the most rapidly evolving areas of generative AI. Veo 3.1 represents a step-change from earlier models: native audio generation alongside video eliminates a major friction point in production workflows. Capabilities are improving quickly — organizations should track this space actively and expect continued advances in clip length, resolution, and controllability.

---

### When to Choose Veo

- When our use case involves video content creation or editing
- Marketing teams that need high volumes of short video content
- Product visualization and demonstration for e-commerce
- Training content that benefits from visual illustration
- Any workflow currently requiring expensive video production for routine content

## Comparison of Google's Foundation Model Family

| | **Gemini** | **Gemma** | **Imagen** | **Veo** |
|--|-----------|----------|-----------|--------|
| **Primary modality** | Multimodal (text, images, audio, video, code) | Text (code and vision variants available) | Text → Image | Text/Image → Video |
| **Open or proprietary** | Proprietary | Open-weight | Proprietary | Proprietary |
| **Key strength** | Maximum capability, long context, multimodal reasoning | Data control, on-premises, full customization | High-quality image generation and editing | Video generation |
| **Primary deployment** | Agent Platform API, Google products | Download and self-host, or Agent Platform | Agent Platform API, Google products | Agent Platform API, Google products |
| **Best for** | Complex tasks, multimodal workflows, Google ecosystem | Regulated industries, privacy-sensitive, heavy fine-tuning | Marketing visuals, product imagery, creative content | Video content production, marketing, demonstrations |
| **Requires internal infrastructure** | No | Yes | No | No |
| **Per-use cost** | Yes (token-based) | Infrastructure cost only | Yes (request-based) | Yes (per clip/second) |
| **Fine-tuning** | Via Agent Platform API | Direct weight access | Limited | Limited |
| **Choose when** | Maximum performance, multimodal, Google integration | Data cannot leave our environment | Visual content creation | Video content creation |

---

## Practical Guidance: Matching Models to Business Scenarios

**Scenario A — A financial services firm wants to analyze regulatory filings, which are long PDFs containing text, tables, and charts:**
Gemini — multimodal, with a long context window to process full documents, and the ability to analyze charts and text together in an integrated way.

**Scenario B — A healthcare provider needs an AI assistant for clinical staff, but patient data cannot leave their private cloud:**
Gemma — open-weight, deployed on their own secure infrastructure, so no data leaves their environment and they maintain full HIPAA compliance.

**Scenario C — A consumer brand's marketing team needs to generate product lifestyle images for 500 SKUs across 15 different room settings:**
Imagen — text-to-image generation at scale, available via Agent Platform API for integration into production content workflows.

**Scenario D — A retail company wants short video clips demonstrating each product category for their e-commerce site:**
Veo 3.1 — video generation from product descriptions and imagery, with native audio generation for narration and sound effects, dramatically reducing production costs at scale.

**Scenario E — A software company wants to integrate an AI coding assistant into their development environment, but all source code is proprietary and sensitive:**
CodeGemma — an open-weight code model that can be deployed on internal infrastructure, so source code never leaves the organization.

**Scenario F — A multinational corporation needs a general-purpose AI assistant for knowledge workers across all departments:**
Gemini for Google Workspace — a pre-built application layer integrated across Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Slides, enabling broad adoption without custom development.

No single model is the right answer for every use case. The thoughtful approach is to match model capabilities to the specific requirements of each use case, considering data sensitivity, content type, performance requirements, and cost.
