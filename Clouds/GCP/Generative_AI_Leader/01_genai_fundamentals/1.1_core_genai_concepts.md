# 1.1 Core GenAI Concepts

Understanding generative AI starts with a clear vocabulary. The terms below appear constantly in discussions, vendor pitches, and product documentation. Knowing what they actually mean — and what the practical implications are for our organization — separates informed decision-makers from those who are easily misled by jargon.

---

## Core AI Terms

### Artificial Intelligence (AI)
**Artificial intelligence** is the broad field of computer science concerned with building systems that can perform tasks that would normally require human intelligence: understanding language, recognizing patterns, making decisions, learning from experience, and solving problems.

The term "AI" covers an enormous range of technologies — from a simple rule-based chatbot that follows decision trees to a large language model capable of nuanced reasoning. When vendors say "AI-powered," the term tells us almost nothing by itself. The important questions are: what specific AI technique is being used, and what is it actually doing?

---

### Natural Language Processing (NLP)
**Natural language processing (NLP)** is the branch of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language — the kind of language people use in everyday conversation, documents, and speech.

NLP covers a wide spectrum of tasks:
- **Text classification:** Is this email positive or negative? Is this review a complaint?
- **Entity extraction:** Pull out all company names, dates, and dollar figures from this contract.
- **Sentiment analysis:** What is the emotional tone of these customer survey responses?
- **Translation:** Convert this document from Spanish to English.
- **Question answering:** Given this document, answer the questions about it.
- **Summarization:** Give me a one-paragraph summary of this 50-page report.

Large language models like Gemini are the most powerful NLP systems ever built. They have absorbed so much language that they can handle all of the above tasks and many more through a single interface — a natural language prompt.

*Why it matters:* The majority of business information lives in language — emails, contracts, reports, meeting notes, customer messages, documentation. NLP is the technology that makes this information accessible to AI systems.

---

### Machine Learning (ML)
**Machine learning** is a type of AI where systems learn from data rather than following explicitly programmed rules. Instead of a developer writing out all the logic ("if the email contains 'Nigerian prince,' mark it as spam"), a machine learning system is trained on examples ("here are 100,000 emails labeled spam or not-spam — learn the patterns yourself").

The distinction from traditional software is fundamental:

| Traditional Software | Machine Learning |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| Developer writes explicit rules | System learns rules from examples |
| Behavior is fully specified upfront | Behavior emerges from training data |
| Changing behavior requires code changes | Changing behavior requires new training data |
| Predictable but inflexible | Adaptable but sometimes opaque |

Machine learning is the engine under the hood of virtually all modern AI — including generative AI. The "learning" in machine learning refers to the process of adjusting millions (or billions) of numerical parameters based on training examples until the system performs well on the task.

*Business implication:* ML systems are only as good as their training data. A fraud detection model trained on data from five years ago may miss new fraud patterns. A customer segmentation model trained on pre-pandemic data may produce outdated segments. Data quality and recency matter enormously.

---

### Generative AI
**Generative AI** refers to AI systems that can create new content — text, images, audio, video, code, or other data — rather than just classifying or predicting from existing content.

What makes it "generative" is that the output is genuinely new. The model is not retrieving a stored answer or selecting from a predefined set of options. It is producing original content that did not exist before the prompt was given.

The mechanism varies by content type:
- **Text:** Large language models predict the most likely continuation of a sequence of tokens
- **Images:** Diffusion models start with random noise and progressively refine it toward a coherent image matching the description
- **Audio:** Autoregressive models or diffusion models produce waveforms matching spoken language or musical descriptions

*Business implication:* Generative AI shifts the role of AI from "analyzer and classifier" to "creator and collaborator." It can draft the email, not just classify whether an email is urgent. It can generate the product image, not just detect whether an image is appropriate.

#### The Four Primary Value Drivers of Generative AI
Enterprise applications generally fall into four primary capabilities: **Create, Summarize, Discover, and Automate**. 

##### 1. Create
Using generative AI to build net-new content across multiple modalities, reducing the time from concept to execution.
* **Writing text and communication:** Draft articles, emails, and social media posts. This includes generating text responses to customer service inquiries via **Conversational AI**, or drafting client emails natively using **Gemini in Gmail**.
* **Creating images, videos, and audio:** Build custom images for presentations using **Gemini in Google Slides**, generate multiple design concepts for brainstorming marketing materials via **Imagen**, or build video tutorials to guide customers through troubleshooting steps with **Google Vids**.
* **Generating code:** Produce code as a starting point for software development projects using **Gemini Code Assist**, and generate unit tests or test cases to reduce manual testing overhead while ensuring software stability.

##### 2. Summarize
Condensing vast datasets and long-form documents into digestible, high-impact insights to speed up decision-making.
* **Summarizing long documents and articles:** Extract core information from lengthy financial reports without reading the entire document using **NotebookLM Enterprise**, or aggregate customer reviews to understand product feedback and sentiment via **Gemini in Looker**.
* **Extracting meeting and presentation takeaways:** Automatically generate meeting notes that highlight key decisions and action items in **Gemini in Google Meet**, or quickly recap client calls for sales teams to identify immediate next steps.
* **Creating concise reports from complex data:** Transform complex datasets into easily understandable visual assets, such as graphs, using **Gemini in BigQuery**.

##### 3. Discover
Helping customers and employees find the exact information or hidden patterns they need at the right time.
* **Uncovering hidden data insights:** Analyze purchase history and website traffic to predict future demand using **forecasting models on Vertex AI**, optimize subscriber engagement and reduce churn through viewing pattern analysis with **Vertex AI Search**, or evaluate massive molecular structures to identify high-success drug candidates by **fine-tuning models on Vertex AI**.
* **Searching resources and documentation:** Query individual files or ask nuanced questions about their contents using **Gemini in Google Drive**. At scale, **Gemini Enterprise** unlocks institutional expertise via intelligent agents that combine Gemini’s reasoning with Google-quality search across all hosted enterprise data.
* **Monitoring real-time events:** Leverage anomaly detection models to identify fraudulent transactions instantaneously and prevent financial losses.

##### 4. Automate
Streamlining and executing business processes that previously required repetitive human intervention.
* **Automated format conversation:** Convert content into accessible formats in real time using Google Cloud's core APIs, including **Text-to-Speech**, **Speech-to-Text**, and **Translation**.
* **Automated documentation and extraction:** Analyze source code to automatically generate clear, consistent documentation using **Codey**, or streamline contract review and information extraction via **Document AI**.
* **Automated notifications and alarms:** Conduct automated customer feedback analysis to trigger immediate support ticket creation and operational alerts.

### Foundation Models
A **foundation model** is a large AI model trained on broad, diverse data at scale, which can then be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks. The term "foundation" captures the key idea: this model serves as the base upon which many different applications are built.

Before foundation models, the standard approach was to train a separate, specialized model for each task. Foundation models changed this:

**Before foundation models:**
- Train model A to classify customer support emails by topic
- Train model B to summarize legal contracts
- Train model C to answer questions from product documentation
- Three separate projects, three separate datasets, three separate training runs

**After foundation models:**
- Start with Gemini (already trained on vast data)
- Connect it to our customer support emails, our contracts, our documentation
- Configure it via prompts or fine-tuning for each use case
- Much faster, cheaper, and more flexible

The paradigm shift is profound: the most expensive and technically demanding part — the initial training on billions of examples — is done once (by Google, or another AI company) and then shared with everyone. Organizations can now build sophisticated AI applications without a large machine learning research team.

*Business analogy:* Think of a foundation model like a highly trained generalist consultant who has read millions of documents across dozens of industries. They arrive on day one with enormous general knowledge. Our job is not to educate them from scratch — it is to orient them toward our specific context and goals.

---

### Multimodal Foundation Models
A **multimodal** model is one that can accept and produce multiple types of content — text, images, audio, video, code — rather than being limited to a single modality.

Earlier AI systems were specialized: a computer vision model processed images but couldn't understand text about those images. A language model processed text but couldn't analyze a photograph. Multimodal models break down these silos.

**What multimodality enables:**
- Show the model a picture of a broken product and ask "what is wrong with this?" — the model understands both the visual content and the textual question
- Provide an audio recording and ask for a written summary with key action items
- Give the model a chart and ask it to explain the trend and draft an email to the team
- Process a document that contains both text and embedded images as a unified whole

Google's Gemini was designed from the ground up to be natively multimodal — meaning it doesn't simply apply separate text and image models and stitch together the results. It reasons across modalities in an integrated way.

*Business implication:* Real-world business processes involve multiple content types. Customer communications include text, images, and voice. Contracts are PDFs with text and tables. Products have photos, specifications (text), and video demonstrations. Multimodal AI can handle these naturally without requiring our team to pre-process everything into a single format.

### Diffusion Models
**Diffusion models** are the architecture behind most modern image generation AI (and increasingly, video and audio generation). Rather than drawing an image from scratch, they work through a process of refinement.

**Conceptual explanation (non-technical):**

Imagine starting with a completely blurry, random static image — pure noise. Now imagine applying a sophisticated filter that, step by step, makes the image slightly more coherent and slightly more aligned with the description "a red barn on a snowy hillside at sunset." After many such steps, the noise has resolved into a recognizable, detailed image.

During training, the model learns the reverse process: how to "denoise" — that is, how to transform random noise into structured, meaningful images that match descriptions. After training on millions of image-text pairs, the model has learned the visual patterns of the world well enough to apply them to new descriptions it has never seen.

*Why it matters for business leaders:* We do not need to understand the mathematics. What matters is that diffusion models produce images of surprisingly high quality from text descriptions, enable image editing from text instructions ("make the sky more dramatic," "remove the background"), and are the engine behind tools like Google's Imagen. The business opportunity is in image creation workflows — marketing, product visualization, design prototyping — that previously required professional designers or expensive stock photography.

### Large Language Models (LLMs)
A **large language model (LLM)** is a type of deep learning model trained on enormous amounts of text, capable of understanding and generating human language with remarkable fluency and versatility.

**How they work:**

1. **Training:** The model is exposed to a vast corpus of text — books, web pages, articles, code, and more. During training, it learns to predict what comes next in a sequence of text. From doing this billions of times, it builds up a rich mathematical representation of language, knowledge, and reasoning patterns.

2. **Representation:** The model does not store text like a database. It compresses the patterns of language into billions of numerical parameters. These parameters capture relationships: that "Paris" relates to "France" the way "London" relates to "England," that questions of a certain form tend to have answers of a certain form, that certain words carry emotional valence.

3. **Generation:** When we give the model a prompt, it processes our input and generates a response one token at a time (a token is roughly a word or part of a word). At each step, it considers all the context available to it and predicts what token most usefully continues the response. It does this repeatedly until the response is complete.

4. **Instruction following:** Modern LLMs are further trained using human feedback to follow instructions, be helpful, and avoid harmful outputs. This is what makes them practical assistants rather than just text predictors.

**What distinguishes "large" in LLMs:**
- Scale of training data (hundreds of billions of words)
- Number of parameters (billions to hundreds of billions)
- Emergent capabilities — abilities that appear at scale and weren't explicitly trained for, like multi-step reasoning, analogy-making, and following complex instructions

*Business analogy:* An LLM is like an extraordinarily well-read person who has absorbed the written output of human civilization — and who can engage with us about almost any topic, help us write almost anything, and reason through problems using what they've absorbed. The limitation is that their knowledge has a cutoff date, they can't verify facts in real time, and their "memory" (context window) within a conversation is finite.

### Prompt Engineering
**Prompt engineering** is the practice of crafting inputs to a generative AI model to get more accurate, useful, and consistent outputs.

Because generative AI models respond to natural language, the way we phrase our request significantly influences the quality of the response. A vague prompt produces a vague response; a well-structured prompt produces a specific, useful one.

**Examples of prompt engineering techniques:**
- **Role specification:** "We are a senior legal advisor reviewing a commercial lease. Identify any clauses that present unusual risk to the tenant."
- **Few-shot examples:** Provide two or three examples of the format we want before making our request
- **Chain-of-thought prompting:** Ask the model to "think through this step by step" before giving an answer — this improves accuracy on complex reasoning tasks
- **Constraints:** "Respond in bullet points. Use no more than 150 words. Avoid technical jargon."
- **Context provision:** Include the relevant background information the model needs to give a good answer

**Why it matters organizationally:**
Well-designed prompts are organizational assets. A carefully crafted prompt that reliably produces high-quality first drafts of a particular document type is worth capturing and sharing, not re-invented by each employee. Forward-thinking organizations are creating prompt libraries and best practices as part of their AI enablement programs.

*Business implication:* The skill of working effectively with AI is increasingly a workplace competency. Organizations that invest in training employees to prompt effectively will see better returns on their AI investments.

---

### Prompt Tuning
**Prompt tuning** is a lightweight model customization technique that learns a set of optimized "soft prompts" — small sets of parameters prepended to inputs — that steer the model toward better performance on a specific task, without modifying the underlying model weights.

Think of it this way: instead of manually writing a better prompt (prompt engineering), the system automatically learns which prompt-like instructions, expressed as numerical parameters, consistently produce better results for our task.

**How it compares to full fine-tuning:**
- **Fine-tuning:** Update the model's weights on our data. More powerful but more expensive, requires more data, and produces a separate model copy.
- **Prompt tuning:** Learn optimal "virtual prompts" to prepend. Far less compute-intensive, the base model stays unchanged, and multiple tuned variants can share one underlying model.

*When to consider prompt tuning:* When prompt engineering alone isn't achieving the performance we need, but full fine-tuning is too expensive or when we lack sufficient training data for fine-tuning.

## ML Approaches: Supervised, Unsupervised, and Reinforcement Learning
Machine learning is not one monolithic technique. The three core approaches differ fundamentally in how the system learns.

### Supervised Learning
**Supervised learning** trains a model on labeled examples — input-output pairs where the correct answer is provided — so the model learns to produce the correct output for new inputs.

The "supervision" is the labels: a human (or automated process) has annotated the training data with the right answers.

**How it works:**
- Give the model thousands of emails labeled "spam" or "not spam"
- The model learns which patterns correlate with each label
- When shown a new, unlabeled email, it predicts which label applies

**Business examples:**
| Application | Input | Label (Supervised Signal) |
|-------------|-------|--------------------------|
| Fraud detection | Transaction features | Fraudulent / not fraudulent |
| Loan approval | Applicant financial data | Approved / denied |
| Medical diagnosis | X-ray image | Disease present / absent |
| Sentiment analysis | Customer review text | Positive / neutral / negative |
| Document classification | Contract text | Contract type (NDA, SLA, etc.) |

**Strengths:** High accuracy on the specific task; predictable behavior; results are interpretable.

**Limitations:** Requires labeled training data, which is often expensive to create; model only generalizes to the distribution it was trained on; doesn't work without labeled examples.

*Business implication:* Supervised learning powers most traditional business AI — fraud detection, churn prediction, quality control, compliance screening. These tasks benefit from its precision when labeled data is available.

---

### Unsupervised Learning
**Unsupervised learning** trains a model on data without labels, allowing it to discover patterns and structure on its own — without being told what to look for.

The system must find the structure in the data without guidance on what "correct" looks like.

**How it works:**
- Give the model a million customer purchase records without any labels
- The model finds natural groupings: some customers always buy premium products, some buy only on sale, some are high-frequency small-basket shoppers
- These groups emerge from the data patterns, not from pre-defined categories

**Business examples:**
| Application | What It Finds |
|-------------|---------------|
| Customer segmentation | Natural groups of customers with similar behavior patterns |
| Anomaly detection | Transactions or system events that deviate significantly from normal patterns |
| Topic modeling | Common themes across thousands of support tickets (without pre-defining the topics) |
| Recommendation systems | Latent preferences by finding users with similar taste profiles |
| Data compression | Compact representations that preserve the most important information |

**Strengths:** No labeling required; can discover unexpected patterns; scales to very large datasets.

**Limitations:** Results can be harder to interpret; we don't always know if the discovered patterns are meaningful; evaluation is harder without ground truth.

*Business implication:* Unsupervised learning is valuable for exploratory analysis — discovering what questions to ask rather than answering pre-specified ones. Customer segmentation, anomaly detection, and finding patterns in large unstructured datasets are prime applications.

---

### Reinforcement Learning
**Reinforcement learning (RL)** trains a system through trial and error, with rewards for good outcomes and penalties for poor ones — the system learns to maximize reward over time.

Rather than learning from a static dataset, an RL system learns by interacting with an environment and receiving feedback.

**How it works:**
- An AI agent takes an action
- The environment gives a reward signal (positive for good outcomes, negative for bad ones)
- Over many iterations, the agent learns which actions lead to higher cumulative rewards
- No labeled examples are needed — just a reward signal

**Business examples:**
| Application | Agent Action | Reward Signal |
|-------------|--------------|---------------|
| Recommendation systems | Which product to show next | User clicks / purchases |
| Robotics / warehouse automation | Physical movements | Task completion without collision |
| Ad bidding | Bid amount for each impression | Revenue from resulting conversions |
| Game-playing AI | Game moves | Winning / scoring points |
| ChatGPT/Gemini training | Model responses | Human preference ratings |

That last example is important: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is a technique used in training LLMs to be more helpful, harmless, and honest. Human raters compare model responses and express preferences; this feedback trains a reward model, which is then used to further train the LLM.

**Strengths:** Can achieve superhuman performance on well-defined tasks; discovers non-obvious strategies; works when it's easier to evaluate outcomes than to specify how to achieve them.

**Limitations:** Requires a well-defined reward signal; can find unexpected ways to maximize reward that aren't what we intended ("reward hacking"); training can be unstable; harder to apply to open-ended tasks.

## Types of Generative AI Output
Generative AI can create multiple categories of content, each with distinct business applications.

### Text Generation
The most mature and widely deployed. Includes writing assistance, summarization, translation, question answering, conversational AI, and report generation.

*Business applications:* Email drafting, customer service chatbots, contract summarization, policy documentation, marketing copy, meeting notes.

### Image Generation
AI models that create realistic or stylized images from text descriptions. Google's Imagen is a leading example.

*Business applications:* Marketing content creation, product visualization, interior design mockups, advertising visuals, custom illustrations without needing a graphic designer for every asset.

### Code Generation
Models trained on large code repositories that can write, explain, debug, and translate software code.

*Business applications:* Accelerating developer productivity, generating boilerplate code, explaining legacy code, translating between programming languages, automated code review suggestions.

### Video Generation
AI that generates video clips from text prompts or images. Google's Veo is part of this space.

*Business applications:* Marketing video production, product demonstrations, training material creation, entertainment content.

### Audio and Speech Generation
Models that generate realistic human speech, music, or sound effects. Applications include text-to-speech, voice cloning, and music generation.

*Business applications:* Automated call center voice responses, podcast content creation, language learning tools, accessibility features for visually impaired users.

### Multimodal AI
Systems that accept and generate multiple content types in an integrated, fluid workflow.

*Business applications:* Customer support systems handling voice, text, and images; document analysis combining charts with text; processing multimedia communications.

## Traditional AI vs. Generative AI — A Comparison

| Aspect | Traditional / Narrow AI | Generative AI |
|--------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Primary function** | Classify, predict, detect, rank | Create, generate, compose, converse |
| **Training approach** | Labeled examples for a specific task | Self-supervised learning on massive, broad data |
| **Flexibility** | Highly optimized for one task; cannot generalize | A single model handles many different tasks |
| **Output type** | A prediction, score, or category (e.g., fraud probability = 91%) | A full creative output (a written document, image, code) |
| **Reliability** | Very high for its specific task | High overall; can produce incorrect outputs (hallucination) |
| **Interface** | Usually requires structured input (form fields, database query) | Natural language — anyone can use it |
| **Training data scale** | Thousands to millions of labeled examples | Billions to trillions of examples |
| **Model size** | Thousands to millions of parameters | Billions to hundreds of billions of parameters |
| **Examples** | Spam filter, fraud detection, image classifier | Gemini, Imagen, Veo, GitHub Copilot |
| **Best for** | Well-defined, high-stakes prediction tasks | Creative, generative, open-ended, conversational tasks |

### When to Use Each

**Choose traditional AI when:**
- We need very high precision on a specific, well-defined task
- Incorrect predictions have serious consequences (fraud, medical diagnosis, compliance)
- We have abundant labeled data for that specific task
- We need fully auditable, explainable decisions

**Choose generative AI when:**
- The task is creative, compositional, or open-ended
- We need a flexible interface (natural language) rather than structured inputs
- A wide variety of tasks need to be handled without building separate models for each
- Content creation, summarization, dialogue, or synthesis is the goal

Many sophisticated enterprise AI systems combine both: a generative AI frontend for natural language interaction, backed by traditional AI models for precision tasks like fraud scoring, with human oversight at key decision points.
