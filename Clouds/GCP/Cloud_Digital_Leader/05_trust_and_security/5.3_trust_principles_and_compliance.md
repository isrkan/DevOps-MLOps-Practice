## Section 5.3 — Google Cloud's Trust Principles and Compliance

### Google Cloud's Seven Trust Principles
Google Cloud has formally published seven trust principles that define its commitments to customers. These are not just marketing statements — they are backed by contracts, third-party audits, and legal positions that Google has taken in court.

| # | Trust Principle | What It Means for Your Business |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **You own your data** | Your data belongs to you. Google does not claim any rights to customer data. You can export or delete it at any time. |
| **2** | **Google does not sell your data to third parties** | Unlike advertising-supported services, Google Cloud does not monetize your business data. Your customer records are not sold. |
| **3** | **Google Cloud does not use your data for advertising** | Your cloud data stays in your cloud environment. It is not used to target you or your customers with Google ads. |
| **4** | **All customer data is encrypted by default** | At rest and in transit, automatically, with no configuration required from the customer. |
| **5** | **We guard against insider access to your data** | Google minimizes administrative access to your data. Through tools like Access Transparency, you receive near real-time logs if a Google engineer ever accesses your content for support or maintenance. |
| **6** | **Google will guard against government backdoor access** | If a government demands access to your data, Google notifies you (where legally permitted) and challenges overreaching requests in court. Google will not intentionally create vulnerabilities or backdoors at the request of any government or third party. |
| **7** | **Google's privacy practices are audited by independent third parties** | These commitments are verified externally — not just self-reported. |

> **Key Point:** Principle #5 is particularly important for multinational organizations concerned about government surveillance. Google publishes transparency reports showing how many government data requests it receives, how many it complies with, and how many it challenges in court.

### Transparency Reports and Third-Party Audits

#### Transparency Reports
Google publishes **Transparency Reports** — regular public disclosures that show:
- How many government data requests Google received (broken down by country and type)
- How many of those requests Google complied with
- How many Google challenged or refused
- How many times Google notified users about requests (where legally permitted)

**Why this matters to enterprises:** A company entrusting sensitive customer data or intellectual property to a cloud provider reasonably wants to know: "If a government demands access to my data, what will Google do?" The Transparency Report provides the historical record. It shows Google has challenged many overreaching government requests — including fighting all-writs orders and national security letters in US courts.

#### Third-Party Audits
Google Cloud undergoes regular independent audits by accredited third-party audit firms who verify that Google's security controls actually work as claimed. These auditors have full access to Google's data centers, systems, and documentation.

**Why third-party audits matter:**
- No enterprise or government agency can audit Google themselves — that would be impractical and Google could not grant such access to every customer
- Instead, a single accredited audit firm conducts the audit and issues a formal report that all customers can rely on
- Regulated industries require their cloud vendors to demonstrate compliance with audit reports, not just assertions

**Analogy:** When a company's financial statements are audited by an independent accountant (PricewaterhouseCoopers, Deloitte), investors can rely on those statements without auditing the company themselves. Cloud audit reports work the same way — a trusted independent party verifies the controls so that all customers benefit from a single audit.

### Data Sovereignty and Data Residency

#### Data Sovereignty
**Data sovereignty** is the legal principle that data is governed by the laws of the country where it is physically stored or processed. When data crosses international borders — even temporarily — it may become subject to the laws of the country it passes through.

**Why this creates complexity:** A French company's customer data stored in a US-based cloud data center is subject to US laws, including the CLOUD Act (Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data Act), which allows US law enforcement to compel US cloud providers to produce data stored anywhere in the world. This may conflict with the EU's GDPR, which restricts the transfer of EU citizens' data outside the European Economic Area.

**Real business impact:** A German bank subject to BaFin (German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) regulations may be legally prohibited from storing customer financial data outside Germany. A French government ministry may be required to use only EU-based cloud infrastructure. A healthcare company may need to keep patient data within national borders.

#### Data Residency
**Data residency** is the physical location where data is stored. Organizations increasingly need control over exactly where their data lives.

**Google Cloud's data residency controls:**

| Tool | What it does | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **Region selection** | Choose exactly which GCP region(s) store your data at deployment time | Any organization with geographic data requirements |
| **Assured Workloads** | Restricts which regions store data AND restricts which Google employees (by geography) can access it for support | Regulated industries; US government contractors; financial services |
| **Access Transparency** | Logs every time a Google employee accesses your customer data, with the business justification | Organizations requiring audit trails of cloud provider access |
| **Sovereign Cloud** | Full data isolation for governments — data never leaves the country; operated by a local partner | National governments; intelligence agencies; critical infrastructure |

**Assured Workloads in detail:** When Assured Workloads is configured for EU regions, it restricts your data to European data centers and also restricts which Google support engineers can access your environment — only EU-based employees with appropriate clearances. This satisfies both GDPR data residency and data sovereignty requirements for EU-regulated entities.

> **Key Point:** Data residency tells you *where* your data is stored. Data sovereignty is the *legal framework* that governs that data. Organizations often need both — to keep data in a specific country AND to ensure it is governed by that country's laws.

### Compliance Frameworks and Google Cloud Resources

#### Key Compliance Frameworks
Each compliance framework exists for a specific reason and applies to specific types of organizations:

| Framework | Full Name | Who Must Comply | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISO 27001** | International Organization for Standardization 27001 | Any organization wanting internationally recognized security certification | Information security management systems; controls for protecting data |
| **SOC 2** | Service Organization Control 2 | Cloud service providers; SaaS companies | Security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy of cloud services |
| **PCI DSS** | Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard | Any organization that processes, stores, or transmits credit card data | Protection of cardholder data; required by Visa, Mastercard, and other card networks |
| **HIPAA** | Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act | US healthcare providers, insurers, and their vendors | Protection of patient health information (PHI) |
| **GDPR** | General Data Protection Regulation | Any organization handling EU citizens' personal data (regardless of where the organization is based) | Rights of EU citizens over their personal data; restrictions on processing and transfer |
| **FedRAMP** | Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program | Cloud service providers serving US federal government agencies | Standardized security requirements for federal cloud deployments |

**Important nuance about GDPR:** GDPR does not only apply to European companies. An American retailer that sells to customers in Germany and collects their personal data must comply with GDPR. The regulation follows the data subject (the EU citizen), not the organization's location.

#### Google Cloud Compliance Resource Center
The **Google Cloud Compliance Resource Center** is a publicly accessible hub that provides:
- Documentation explaining how Google Cloud services support each compliance framework
- Mapping of GCP services to specific compliance controls (e.g., "Cloud Storage is in-scope for PCI DSS")
- Guides for configuring GCP to meet specific compliance requirements
- Explanations of Google's compliance certifications and their scope

This resource allows an organization's compliance team to understand exactly which GCP services can be used for regulated workloads and which configuration settings are required.

#### Compliance Reports Manager
The **Compliance Reports Manager** allows Google Cloud customers to download Google's official third-party audit reports directly from the Cloud Console — without needing to contact Google, fill out a form, or wait for a response.

**What reports are available:**
- ISO 27001 and ISO 27017/27018 certificates
- SOC 1, SOC 2, and SOC 3 reports
- PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance
- FedRAMP Authorization Package
- HIPAA Business Associate Agreement

**The business value of Compliance Reports Manager:** When an enterprise customer, partner, or regulator asks "Can you provide evidence that your cloud provider meets our security standards?", the answer is to download the relevant report from Compliance Reports Manager and share it. This process that previously required weeks of back-and-forth with vendor management teams now takes minutes.

**Analogy:** Instead of scheduling a personal tour of a restaurant's kitchen to verify food safety compliance, you simply download the health department's inspection report — a trusted third-party document that verifies the same information more efficiently.

> **Key Point:** The exam often tests the distinction between these two resources. The **Compliance Resource Center** explains what compliance frameworks exist and how GCP supports them. The **Compliance Reports Manager** provides the actual audit reports and certificates you can share with auditors.
