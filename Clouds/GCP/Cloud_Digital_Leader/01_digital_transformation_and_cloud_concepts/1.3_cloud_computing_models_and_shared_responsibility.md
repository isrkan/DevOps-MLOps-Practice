## Section 1.3 — Cloud Computing Models and Shared Responsibility

### IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS: The Service Model Spectrum
The three cloud service models — IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS — represent different points on a spectrum from "we manage almost everything" to "the provider manages almost everything." Understanding these models is essential for choosing the right approach for each business need. In cloud computing, the cloud service provider owns, manages, and maintains the resources.

#### Abstraction
Before looking at different cloud models, we must understand the concept of **abstraction**. In a technical context, abstraction means hiding complex, technical background details behind a simple interface.

Cloud computing operates on this exact principle. Instead of requiring engineering teams to manage data center power grids, physical storage arrays, or hardware network switches, the cloud provider handles the underlying physical infrastructure. The provider then delivers a clean digital interface—such as a web console or an API endpoint—allowing organizations to provision and manage global resources instantly.

#### What Does "As a Service" Mean?
Technical abstraction enables the *"As a Service" (aaS) delivery model. In enterprise technology, delivering something "as a service" simply means renting access to a resource over the internet instead of buying, owning, and maintaining it yourself.

---

#### IaaS — Infrastructure as a Service
**What it is:** The cloud provider gives us virtualized computing resources — virtual machines (VMs), storage, and networking — which provide the same technologies and capabilities as a traditional data center without the burden of physical maintenance or management. We access these resources over the internet and configure them exactly like physical servers. 

One of the primary reasons businesses choose IaaS is to reduce capital expenditures (CapEx) and transform them into predictable operating expenses (OpEx). Because the cloud provider manages the underlying infrastructure, organizations can concentrate entirely on installing, configuring, and managing software while keeping their data secure. We are responsible for installing the operating system, middleware, and our applications. We manage security at the OS level and above.

**What Google manages:** Physical hardware, data center facilities, power, cooling, networking hardware, virtualization layer.

**What we manage:** Operating system, middleware (web servers, databases), application code, data, security configurations at the OS and application level.

**Google Cloud IaaS products:**
- **Google Compute Engine**: virtual machines we can configure with the OS, CPU, RAM, and storage we specify.
- **Cloud Storage**: A flexible service used to store and secure any type or volume of data.

**Key Benefits of IaaS:**
* **Economical & Efficient:** Eliminates the massive upfront costs of buying physical hardware and reduces wasted spend by converting IT costs into a pay-as-you-go model.
* **Boosts Productivity:** Engineering teams no longer waste time racking servers, running cables, or managing hardware failure logistics, allowing them to focus entirely on deploying software.
* **Reliable & Scalable:** Built on Google’s redundant global infrastructure to minimize downtime, with the built-in capability to scale resources up or out in minutes as business demand changes.

**Best for:**
- Migrating existing on-premises applications to the cloud ("lift and shift") — we can run the same application on a VM in the cloud
- Applications requiring a specific OS version or configuration that managed services do not support
- Enterprises with existing expertise in managing servers who want to move to the cloud but retain control

**Analogy:** Renting a bare apartment. The building owner provides the walls, plumbing, and electricity. We bring our own furniture, appliances, and decorations. We control our space exactly as we want, but we are responsible for furnishing and maintaining it.

---

#### PaaS — Platform as a Service
**What it is:** The cloud provider manages the infrastructure AND the platform — operating system, runtime environment, scaling, load balancing, and middleware. We focus purely on writing and deploying our application code. We do not worry about patching servers or managing scaling.

**What Google manages:** Physical hardware, data center, OS, runtime, scaling, load balancing, security patches for the platform.

**What we manage:** Our application code and our data.

**Google Cloud PaaS products:**
- **Google App Engine**: deploy our web application; Google handles scaling and infrastructure configuration.
* **Cloud Run**: Run containers without managing servers or provisioning infrastructure.
* **BigQuery**: A fully managed, serverless enterprise data warehouse that lets us analyze petabytes of data using SQL without managing database servers.

**Key Benefits of PaaS:**
* **Reduces Development Time:** Streamlines the deployment pipeline, enabling teams to build new features and deliver applications to market faster.
* **Scalable:** Automatically scales resources up and down in real time to match fluctuating user demand without human intervention.
* **Reduces Management:** Eliminates routine system administration, server patching, and infrastructure maintenance burdens.
* **Flexible:** Allows developers to use their preferred programming languages, frameworks, and containerized tools on a highly adaptable platform.

**Best for:**
- Developers who want to focus on building features, not managing infrastructure
- Startups building new applications quickly
- Teams without deep infrastructure expertise
- Applications with variable traffic where automatic scaling is important

**Analogy:** Renting a furnished apartment. The owner provides furniture, appliances, and maintenance. We move in with our personal belongings and live as we choose, but the big infrastructure pieces are handled for us.

---

#### SaaS — Software as a Service
**What it is:** The cloud provider manages everything — infrastructure, platform, and the complete application. We access the application through a web browser or mobile app. We configure settings and use the application, but we have no visibility into or control over the underlying infrastructure.

**What Google manages:** Everything — hardware, OS, application code, scaling, security patches, maintenance, backups.

**What we manage:** Our data within the application and which users have access.

**Google Cloud SaaS products:** 
- **Google Workspace:** A comprehensive suite of cloud-native collaboration and productivity applications (including Gmail, Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet, and Drive) managed entirely by Google and accessed securely from any device.
- **Looker Studio:** A fully managed business intelligence and data visualization platform that allows teams to build interactive dashboards and reports directly from web browsers without managing underlying software or servers.
* **Google Analytics:** A digital analytics software platform delivered entirely as a service to track, analyze, and report website and application traffic patterns globally.

**Other SaaS examples:** Salesforce (CRM), Workday (HR), Zoom (video conferencing), Slack (team messaging).

**Key Benefits of SaaS:**
* **Low Maintenance:** The cloud provider handles all software updates, infrastructure maintenance, bug fixes, and security patches automatically. We never have to manage a rollout or worry about backend stability.
* **Cost-Effective:** Eliminates development, deployment, and capital infrastructure costs. It typically uses a predictable subscription or per-user pricing model, making it easy to budget.
* **Flexible:** We can instantly scale the number of licenses up or down based on our current workforce, and users can securely access the application from any device, anywhere in the world.

**Best for:**
- Standard business productivity tools (email, word processing, video conferencing)
- Functions that are not core to our business (why build our own expense management software when Concur exists?)
- Small businesses without IT departments
- Any application where the vendor's standard offering meets our needs

**Analogy:** Staying in a hotel. Everything is provided and maintained — bed, towels, cleaning service, front desk. We simply show up and use the facility. We make no decisions about the building's maintenance or furnishings.

### Detailed Comparison Table: IaaS vs. PaaS vs. SaaS

| Operational Dimension | Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) | Platform as a Service (PaaS) | Software as a Service (SaaS) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer Responsibility** | Operating system, middleware, application runtimes, data architecture, security configurations. | Application source code, database schemas, and data ingestion. | Data governance, endpoint security, and user access identities. |
| **Google Responsibility** | Physical infrastructure, global networking, storage arrays, virtualization hypervisor. | Physical hardware, operating system abstraction, native runtimes, automated scaling. | Full-stack management (hardware, software orchestration, maintenance, patches). |
| **Architectural Flexibility** | **Highest** — Absolute control over the operating system and software stack configurations. | **Moderate** — Constrained to customer code execution on provider-managed platforms. | **Lowest** — Standardized, turn-key application delivery used out-of-the-box. |
| **Operational Control** | High infrastructure-level granular control. | Moderate platform-level configuration control. | Low application-level administrative control. |
| **Engineering Expertise** | High — Requires specialized systems engineering and administration. | Moderate — Focuses entirely on software development capabilities. | Low — Administered and operated by standard business users. |
| **Deployment Velocity** | Structured — Requires provisioning, OS baselining, and middleware setup. | Rapid — Accelerated pipeline focusing solely on application deployment. | Immediate — On-demand access via user provisioning or subscription. |
| **Cost Architecture** | Provisioned capacity model (paying for active resources and compute runtime). | Consumption or execution-driven compute resource model. | Subscription-based (per-user licensing) or usage-based pricing. |
| **Maintenance Overheads** | High — Customer-driven OS patching, security baseline updates, upgrades. | Low — Provider handles platform updates and operating system patching. | Zero — Provider assumes all software lifecycle and maintenance duties. |
| **Strategic Alignment** | Legacy workloads migration, lift-and-shift, highly customized server architectures. | Custom cloud-native development, microservices architectures, rapid prototyping. | Standard business operations, enterprise productivity tools, non-differentiating functions. |
| **Core Google Cloud Examples** | Compute Engine, Cloud Storage | App Engine, Cloud Run, BigQuery | Google Workspace, Looker Studio, Google Analytics |
| **Alternative Enterprise Examples** | Microsoft Azure Virtual Machines, Azure Disk Storage | Microsoft Azure App Services, Azure Functions, Azure Synapse Analytics | Microsoft 365 (Teams, Word, Excel), Dynamics 365 |

### Choosing the Right Model: Business Scenarios

| Business Need | Best Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Small business needs email and document sharing | **SaaS** (Google Workspace) | No IT department needed; immediate value; low cost per user |
| Startup building a custom customer-facing web app | **PaaS** (App Engine) | Developers focus on code; automatic scaling; no server management |
| Enterprise migrating legacy Oracle database to cloud | **IaaS** (Compute Engine) | Need exact same OS/software environment; lift and shift first, modernize later |
| Company needs CRM to manage sales pipeline | **SaaS** (Salesforce) | Standard CRM is not a competitive differentiator; buy, don't build |
| Data science team training custom ML models | **IaaS** or **PaaS** | Need specific GPU hardware (IaaS) or managed notebooks (PaaS/Vertex AI) |
| Retailer building a new mobile loyalty app | **PaaS** | Development team can move fast; scaling handled automatically |
| Hospital needs patient scheduling system | **SaaS** or **IaaS** | If standard product exists, SaaS; if heavily customized, IaaS |

### The Shared Responsibility Model
The **shared responsibility model** defines exactly who is responsible for securing and managing each layer of the cloud stack. It is one of the most important concepts in cloud security — and a frequent exam topic.

The key principle: **as we move from IaaS to PaaS to SaaS, Google takes on more responsibility, but we give up more control.** A general guideline for shared responsibility is that "if we configure or store it, we are responsible for securing it."

#### Shared Responsibility Table

| Responsibility | On-Premises | IaaS | PaaS | SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical security of data center** | Customer | **Google** | **Google** | **Google** |
| **Hardware (servers, storage, network)** | Customer | **Google** | **Google** | **Google** |
| **Virtualization / hypervisor** | Customer | **Google** | **Google** | **Google** |
| **Operating system** | Customer | **Customer** | **Google** | **Google** |
| **Network controls (firewalls, VPCs)** | Customer | **Shared** | **Shared** | **Google** |
| **Middleware / runtime** | Customer | **Customer** | **Google** | **Google** |
| **Application code** | Customer | **Customer** | **Customer** | **Google** |
| **Data** | Customer | **Customer** | **Customer** | **Customer** |
| **User identity and access management** | Customer | **Customer** | **Customer** | **Customer** |

> **Key Point:** No matter which service model we use — IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS — WE are always responsible for our **data** and our **users' access** (who is allowed to access what). Google never takes responsibility for what data we put in the cloud or who we give access to.

**Why the shared responsibility model matters:**

A common mistake organizations make when moving to the cloud is assuming "Google handles security now." This is only partially true. Google secures the infrastructure, but we are still responsible for:

- Configuring access controls correctly (not making sensitive data publicly accessible)
- Managing user accounts (disabling accounts when employees leave)
- Encrypting sensitive data at the application level where required
- Patching our application code vulnerabilities
- Configuring our network firewall rules correctly

Google's model is sometimes called **"security of the cloud"** (Google's responsibility) vs. **"security in the cloud"** (our responsibility).
