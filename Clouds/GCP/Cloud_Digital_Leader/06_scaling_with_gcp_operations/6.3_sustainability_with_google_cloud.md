## 6.3 — Sustainability with Google Cloud

### Why Sustainability Matters for Organizations
Sustainability has moved from a "nice to have" to a business imperative:

- **ESG reporting** — investors, regulators, and customers increasingly require organizations to report Environmental, Social, and Governance metrics; cloud IT emissions (Scope 3) must be measured and reported
- **Regulatory pressure** — the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), SEC climate disclosure rules, and similar regulations require detailed carbon accounting
- **Customer and talent expectations** — large enterprise customers include carbon footprint in vendor assessments; top talent (especially engineers) consider employer sustainability practices
- **Cost** — energy efficiency and sustainability often go hand-in-hand; more efficient infrastructure costs less to run

---

### Google Cloud's Sustainability Commitments
Google has the most ambitious sustainability commitments of any major cloud provider:

- **Carbon-neutral since 2007**: Google has offset its carbon emissions for every year since 2007 — longer than any other major technology company. This includes all Google Cloud data centers.
- **100% renewable energy match since 2017**: Google matches its annual global electricity consumption with purchases of renewable energy (solar, wind, hydroelectric). On an annual basis, every kilowatt-hour Google uses is matched by a renewable energy certificate.
- **24/7 Carbon-Free Energy (CFE) by 2030**: This is far more ambitious than annual matching. The goal: power every Google data center with carbon-free energy at every hour of every day, not just on an annual average. This requires matching supply to demand in real time, in every region, which requires significant investment in local renewable energy sources.
    - Progress: as of 2024, Google operates at ~64% 24/7 carbon-free energy globally, with some regions already at 90%+
- **Energy efficiency leadership**: Google's data centers achieve a **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)** of approximately 1.10, versus an industry average of 1.58. PUE measures how much total energy the data center uses relative to IT equipment energy. A perfect score is 1.0 (all energy goes to computing). Google's 1.10 means 10% overhead (cooling, lighting, etc.) vs the industry's 58% overhead.
    - This efficiency means: **running the same workload on Google Cloud uses significantly less energy** than running it in an average enterprise data center, even before accounting for Google's renewable energy purchases.
- **ISO 14001 Certification**: Google maintains third-party ISO 14001 certification for its global data centers. This international standard specifies the requirements for an effective Environmental Management System (EMS). It verifies that Google uses a structured, audited framework to continually improve its environmental performance, optimize resource consumption, minimize waste, and meet compliance obligations across its entire physical infrastructure.

### Google Cloud Products Supporting Sustainability Goals

- **Carbon Footprint (built-in GCP tool)**: Every Google Cloud customer can access a **Carbon Footprint** dashboard in the Cloud Console showing the estimated gross carbon emissions associated with their Google Cloud usage, broken down by: Project, Service (e.g., Compute Engine, BigQuery, Cloud Storage), Region, Month. This data is required for organizations that must report their **Scope 3 emissions** (emissions from purchased services and activities in your value chain, which includes cloud computing). The dashboard provides the numbers you need for ESG reports without any external tooling.
- **Region selection for lower carbon intensity**: Different GCP regions have different proportions of carbon-free energy. Google publishes the **carbon-free energy percentage** for each region. Deploying workloads in low-carbon regions (e.g., regions with high hydroelectric or wind power) reduces your carbon footprint.
- **Carbon-Aware Computing**: For batch workloads (ML training, data processing, rendering) that don't need to run at a specific time, **Carbon-Aware Computing** tools can schedule these jobs to run when and where the carbon intensity of the electricity grid is lowest — automatically shifting work to times and regions with more renewable energy available.
- **Active Assist Recommender (efficiency)**: The same Recommender tool that identifies over-provisioned VMs for cost savings also reduces waste and energy consumption. Eliminating unused resources is both a cost optimization and a sustainability improvement.

### Business Value of Google Cloud Sustainability

- **Reduced carbon footprint** — moving workloads from an average corporate data center (PUE 1.58) to Google Cloud (PUE 1.10) immediately reduces energy consumption by ~30%, plus the benefit of Google's renewable energy purchases
- **Scope 3 emissions reporting** — Carbon Footprint tool provides the data required for ESG and regulatory reporting without additional investment
- **Credible sustainability credentials** — Google's third-party verified sustainability claims are recognized by investors and regulators; using Google Cloud contributes to our own sustainability story
- **Alignment with 2030 goals** — organizations with their own net-zero commitments benefit from a cloud partner whose own 2030 goals are aligned with theirs
