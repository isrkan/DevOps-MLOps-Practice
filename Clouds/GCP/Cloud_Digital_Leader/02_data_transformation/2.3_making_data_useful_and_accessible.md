## Section 2.3 — Making Data Useful and Accessible

### Looker: Democratizing Data Access

#### What Is Looker?
**Looker** is a business intelligence (BI) and data visualization platform that Google Cloud acquired in 2019. It allows non-technical business users to explore data, build dashboards, and generate reports — without writing SQL or waiting for an analyst to run a report.

**The problem Looker solves:** Traditionally, getting insights from data required submitting a request to the data analytics team, waiting 3–5 business days for an analyst to write a SQL query, review the results, and format them into a report. By the time the report arrived, the business context might have changed. Leaders were making decisions based on last week's or last month's data.

**How Looker works:**
1. Data analysts or engineers define a **LookML model** (a business-friendly description of the data: what the tables mean, how they relate, what calculations are standard)
2. Business users access Looker's interface and **explore** data using dropdowns, filters, and clicks — no SQL required
3. Users can build their own dashboards, schedule reports to arrive by email every morning, and embed visualizations in other applications

**Data democratization:** The philosophy that data insights should be available to everyone in an organization — not gatekept by a small team of analysts. Looker makes this real by giving marketing managers, sales reps, operations supervisors, and executives the ability to answer their own data questions.

**Business value:**
- Marketing manager checks yesterday's campaign performance at 8:00 AM without waiting for an analyst
- Sales director creates a custom dashboard showing pipeline by region and rep — updates in real-time
- Operations manager sees current inventory levels by warehouse with one click
- Executive receives an automated weekly email with key business metrics from Looker

#### BigQuery and Looker Integration
The combination of BigQuery (data warehouse) and Looker (BI platform) is a complete, enterprise-grade analytics stack:

1. **Data lives in BigQuery** — all historical data, aggregated from multiple source systems, stored in Google's serverless data warehouse
2. **Looker connects to BigQuery** — Looker sends SQL queries to BigQuery and displays the results as visualizations
3. **Business users use Looker** — they never see BigQuery directly; they interact with Looker's friendly interface

What business users can build:
- **Real-time dashboards** showing live business metrics (refreshes every 5 minutes, pulling from BigQuery)
- **Automated daily reports** delivered by email at 7:00 AM every morning
- **Scheduled email digests** summarizing weekly or monthly performance for executive review
- **Embedded analytics** — Looker visualizations embedded inside our own customer-facing application (a SaaS company might embed Looker dashboards in their product so customers can see their own analytics)

**Business example:** A retail chain has 500 stores. The regional VP for the Northeast wants to see, every morning at 8:00 AM, which stores in her region had the highest and lowest sales the previous day, and how each store compares to its 30-day average. With BigQuery storing all transaction data and Looker providing the dashboard, this report is built once by a data analyst, then runs automatically every morning and is emailed to all regional VPs. No analyst involvement after setup.

### Streaming Analytics: Real-Time Business Value

#### Batch vs. Streaming: The Fundamental Choice
Every data analytics system must choose how to process data: in batches (collected over time, processed together) or as a stream (processed continuously as data arrives). Each approach has different trade-offs.

**Batch Analytics:**
- Data is collected over a period of time (an hour, a day, a week)
- Then processed together in one large job
- Results are hours or days old when they are available
- Cheaper to run (can schedule jobs during off-peak hours)
- Good for: end-of-day sales reports, monthly financial reconciliation, weekly analytics updates

**Streaming Analytics:**
- Data is processed as it arrives — within milliseconds to seconds
- Results are always current
- More complex to build and operate
- More expensive (compute is running continuously)
- Good for: fraud detection, real-time inventory, live dashboards, personalization, alerting

**Analogy:** Batch analytics is like reading yesterday's newspaper — accurate but delayed. Streaming analytics is like watching live news — we see what is happening right now.

---

#### Real-Time Business Value: Where Streaming Analytics Wins
The business value of acting on data in real-time vs. hours later is enormous in several domains:

**Fraud Detection:**
- **Batch approach:** Analyze transactions every night; flag suspicious patterns; contact customers the next day. By then, fraudsters have made 47 more transactions.
- **Streaming approach:** Analyze each transaction as it happens; flag suspicious activity within 2 seconds; automatically decline the transaction and alert the customer. Fraud is stopped before it succeeds.
- **Business impact:** Billions of dollars in prevented fraud losses

**Real-Time Inventory:**
- **Batch approach:** Update inventory counts once per hour; a product goes out of stock at 10:15 AM but the website still shows "In Stock" until 11:00 AM; 45 minutes of disappointed customers who place orders that cannot be fulfilled
- **Streaming approach:** The moment the last unit is sold, the inventory system instantly updates the website to "Out of Stock" and triggers a reorder
- **Business impact:** Better customer experience, fewer order cancellations, more accurate reorder timing

**Personalization:**
- **Batch approach:** Recommend products based on what the customer bought last month; misses what they are clearly shopping for right now
- **Streaming approach:** Observe what the user has searched for and viewed in the last 5 minutes; show recommendations relevant to their current intent
- **Business impact:** Significantly higher conversion rates and average order value

**Operations and Alerting:**
- **Batch approach:** Daily report shows that a server was running at 95% CPU load between 2:00 AM and 3:00 AM; engineers see this at 9:00 AM; the service had degraded for an hour
- **Streaming approach:** The moment CPU exceeds 85%, an alert fires automatically; an engineer investigates within minutes; the issue is resolved before most users experience any impact
- **Business impact:** Higher service availability, better customer experience

### Pub/Sub and Dataflow: Building Modern Data Pipelines

#### Pub/Sub (Publish/Subscribe)
**What it is:** Google Cloud's fully managed messaging service that enables real-time communication between different parts of a system.

**The Pub/Sub model:**
- **Publishers** send messages to a **topic** (a named channel for a particular type of event)
- **Subscribers** listen to a topic and receive messages as they arrive
- Multiple subscribers can receive the same message — each gets their own copy
- If a subscriber is temporarily unavailable, Pub/Sub holds the messages until it recovers

**The radio station analogy:** A radio station (publisher) broadcasts music and news (messages) on a specific frequency (topic). Anyone with a radio (subscriber) tuned to that frequency receives the broadcast. The radio station does not know or care how many listeners there are or what they do with the content they hear.

**Why this matters — decoupling:** In a tightly coupled system, if the payment service needs to notify the inventory service and the shipping service and the billing service about a new order, the payment service must call each of those services directly. If the inventory service is slow, the payment service waits. If the shipping service crashes, the payment service fails.

With Pub/Sub, the payment service publishes a message to the "new-order" topic and is done. The inventory, shipping, and billing services each subscribe to that topic and process the message on their own timeline. If the shipping service crashes, the message waits in Pub/Sub until it recovers — the payment service is unaffected.

**Scale:** Pub/Sub handles millions of messages per second. It is fully managed — no servers to provision, no capacity to plan.

**Use cases:**
- **Event-driven architecture:** Any time a significant business event occurs (new order, payment processed, user signed up), publish a message; all interested systems receive it
- **IoT data ingestion:** 100,000 devices each sending a sensor reading every 30 seconds → Pub/Sub receives all readings reliably and delivers them to processing systems
- **Microservices communication:** Services communicate asynchronously through Pub/Sub without direct dependencies
- **Log aggregation:** Multiple applications publish log events; a central processing system subscribes and processes them

**Business example:** A ride-sharing app uses Pub/Sub as its central nervous system:
- When a rider requests a trip, a message is published to "ride-requests"
- The driver matching service subscribes and finds the nearest driver
- When a driver accepts, a message is published to "ride-accepted"
- The notification service subscribes and sends a push notification to the rider
- The pricing service subscribes and locks in the fare
- The analytics service subscribes and records the event for later analysis

All of this happens in real-time, and each service operates independently. Adding a new service (say, a regulatory compliance reporting service) just requires subscribing to existing topics — no changes to existing services.

#### Dataflow
**What it is:** Google Cloud's fully managed service for processing data streams and batches. Dataflow is where we define what transformations to apply to data as it flows through our pipeline.

**The conveyor belt analogy:** Imagine a factory assembly line. Raw materials come in one end. At each station, something is done to the product: cleaned, assembled, tested, packaged. The finished product comes out the other end ready for shipping. Dataflow is that assembly line for data:
- Raw data comes in (from Pub/Sub, Cloud Storage, BigQuery)
- Transformations are applied at each step: filter out bad records, enrich records with reference data, aggregate values, convert formats
- Clean, transformed data goes out (to BigQuery, Cloud Storage, Pub/Sub, or other systems)

**Technical background:** Dataflow is based on **Apache Beam**, an open-source data processing framework that Google created and donated to the Apache Software Foundation. This means Dataflow pipelines are portable — they can also run on other Beam runners if needed.

**Serverless:** Like BigQuery, Dataflow is serverless. We write our data transformation logic; Google allocates the compute resources needed to run it, scales them up or down based on data volume, and releases them when done. We pay for what we use.

**Batch and streaming, unified:** One of Dataflow's distinctive features is that the same pipeline code can process both batch data (a large historical dataset) and streaming data (real-time events) without modification. Most data processing systems require different code for each. Dataflow's unified model simplifies operations.

**Use cases:**
- **ETL (Extract, Transform, Load):** Extract data from source systems (databases, APIs), transform it (clean, filter, enrich, aggregate), load it into BigQuery for analysis — the most common data engineering task
- **Real-time event processing:** Receive IoT sensor readings from Pub/Sub, calculate rolling averages and anomaly scores, write results to BigQuery and trigger alerts
- **Data validation:** Scan incoming records for quality issues; route bad records to a "dead letter queue" for human review; route good records forward
- **Log processing:** Aggregate and analyze web server logs in real-time; detect attack patterns; update dashboards

**Business example:** A global e-commerce company uses this Dataflow pipeline:
1. Every customer click on the website publishes an event to Pub/Sub
2. Dataflow reads those events in real-time (millions per hour during peak)
3. Dataflow enriches each click with user profile data and product information
4. Dataflow aggregates clicks by product every minute: "Product X has been viewed 1,247 times in the last minute"
5. Dataflow writes results to BigQuery for long-term analysis AND to a real-time dashboard
6. The merchandising team sees which products are trending in real-time — and can update promotions on the fly

**Pub/Sub + Dataflow + BigQuery — the standard modern data pipeline:**

This three-service combination is Google Cloud's recommended architecture for real-time analytics:

```
[Event Sources]
     |
     v
[Pub/Sub] ← Ingests and buffers events reliably
     |
     v
[Dataflow] ← Transforms, enriches, aggregates
     |
     v
[BigQuery] ← Stores for analysis
     |
     v
[Looker] ← Visualizes for business users
```

> **Key Point:** Pub/Sub handles ingestion and reliable delivery. Dataflow handles transformation and processing. BigQuery handles storage and analysis. Looker handles presentation. Each does its job. Together, they form a complete end-to-end real-time analytics system.
